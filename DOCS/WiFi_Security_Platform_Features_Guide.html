🛡️ What is this platform? (The 30-Second Summary)

Wi-Fi networks operate invisibly through the airwaves, making it easy for hackers to set up deceptive traps, listen into unencrypted traffic, or trick your laptop into joining counterfeit hotspots.

This software acts as an automated digital security guard for your wireless environment. It constantly monitors nearby signals, calculates plain-English safety scores, alerts you the moment an attacker creates an "Evil Twin" copy of your network, detects router configuration flaws, optimizes your Wi-Fi channel for speed, and protects the active connection you are currently using—all safely and silently.

🕵️ Feature 1: Evil Twin & Rogue Access Point Detection

MAJOR DEFENSE
💡 What Is It?
An attacker sets up a fake, cloned Wi-Fi router broadcasting the exact same name (SSID) as your real home, university, or office network (e.g. "VIT-Campus" or "HomeWiFi"). If your laptop or phone connects to this clone, the attacker can spy on everything you do online and steal your passwords.
❓ Why Does It Matter?
Wi-Fi devices are designed to remember network names and connect automatically. If an attacker sits nearby with a fake hotspot using your network's name, your device might join it without asking you. This is one of the most common public Wi-Fi cyber attacks.
⚙️ How Does It Work? (Simple Explanation)
Think of it like an employee badge check:
  • Mark as Trusted: You tell the platform your real router once. The tool records its physical hardware address (BSSID), manufacturer (e.g., TP-Link), security type (WPA2), and channel.
  • Forensic Evidence Scoring: On every scan, if it spots a network with your trusted name but coming from an unrecognized router, it calculates an Evil Twin Evidence Score:
    • Same Name, Unknown Hardware Address: +40 points (impersonation detected)
    • Security Weaker than Trusted Version: +30 points (e.g., WPA2 downgraded to Open to lure victims)
    • Different Hardware Manufacturer: +15 points (real is Cisco, clone is a cheap unbranded chip)
    • Different Channel: +5 points (broadcasting on an unexpected frequency)
    • Locally Administered (Fake/Spoofed) MAC: +10 points (indicates software-generated fake MAC)
  • Instant Warning: If the score reaches 40+, it triggers an urgent CRITICAL Alert to stop you from joining!
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Go to Baseline & Trust page → Click ⭐ Mark Selected as Trusted. When an impostor appears, a red alert displays on the Dashboard, Events log, and as a Windows desktop notification.

🛡️ Feature 3: Weak Configuration & Default Password Auditor

VULNERABILITY AUDITOR
💡 What Is It?
An automated audit engine that inspects every Wi-Fi network within radio range and pinpoints dangerous configuration weaknesses—even if no active attack is currently occurring.
❓ Why Does It Matter?
Many routers are left with outdated encryption standards or factory default names. Hackers actively drive around looking for these easy targets because they can be broken into in minutes using automated dictionary attacks.
⚙️ How Does It Work? (Simple Explanation)
Like a security inspector checking doors and window locks:
  • Open Networks (+85 points, CRITICAL): Zero encryption or password. Anyone can read your web traffic.
  • WEP Encryption (+75 points, HIGH): Obsolete 1999 standard that can be cracked in under 60 seconds.
  • Legacy WPA (+35 points, MEDIUM): Outdated security susceptible to handshake recovery.
  • TKIP Cipher (+20 points, MEDIUM): Deprecated encryption algorithm that slows Wi-Fi and has known vulnerabilities.
  • Hidden SSIDs (+15 points, LOW): Routers that hide their name. Hiding provides zero real security because laptops scream the name in public search requests.
  • Default Router Names (+15 points, LOW): Factory names like 'TP-Link_4F2A', 'JioFiber-9912', or 'NETGEAR62'. These reveal the exact router model and usually imply the owner never changed the default admin password!
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Double click any network in Live Scanner → Open the Weak Config tab. Also shown as tags in the Security Monitor table.

📡 Feature 4: Channel Recommendation Engine (2.4 GHz & 5 GHz)

SPEED & STABILITY
💡 What Is It?
An intelligent radio frequency advisor that tests Wi-Fi airwave congestion and tells you the exact channel number your router should switch to for maximum speed and zero interference.
❓ Why Does It Matter?
Wi-Fi travels through radio frequencies like highway traffic. On the 2.4 GHz band, channels heavily overlap with each other. If your neighbor is on channel 6 and you are on channel 5, your routers will talk over each other, causing lag, high ping in games, and dropped video calls.
⚙️ How Does It Work? (Simple Explanation)
A smart traffic GPS for your Wi-Fi:
  • The 2.4 GHz Problem: Only channels 1, 6, and 11 do not physically overlap. All intermediate channels collide. The engine calculates an exact Congestion Score for 1, 6, and 11 by adding the signal strength of all routers sharing the channel, plus 70% of the signals from overlapping neighboring channels.
  • The 5 GHz Advantage: 5 GHz channels do not overlap! The engine tallies how many networks exist on channels 36, 40, 44, 48, etc., and finds the quietest frequency.
  • Recommendation Output: Gives a clear verdict: "Channel 6 is congested (9 networks, score 480). Channel 11 is clean (1 network, score 45). Recommended: Switch router to Channel 11."
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Navigate to Analytics & Signals → Look at the prominent card at the top titled Channel Recommendation Engine with live 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz badges.

🔒 Feature 5: Active Connection Integrity Guard (ARP & DNS Watchdog)

ACTIVE SESSION GUARD
💡 What Is It?
A dedicated watchdog that guards the Wi-Fi network you are currently connected to, protecting you against Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attacks and fake DNS manipulation.
❓ Why Does It Matter?
Scanning other networks is great, but what about the network you are actively using right now? If a hacker on the same Wi-Fi performs an ARP poisoning attack or alters your DNS server, they can silently divert your banking and email traffic to counterfeit phishing websites.
⚙️ How Does It Work? (Simple Explanation)
Two continuous integrity checks running quietly under the hood:
  • ARP Spoofing Detection: When you connect, your computer talks to your router (the Default Gateway). The monitor memorizes the router's hardware MAC address. If an attacker tries to trick your laptop into sending traffic to them instead (changing the gateway MAC while on the same network), the tool triggers an immediate CRITICAL Tamper Alert.
  • DNS Hijacking Detection: When you type 'google.com', your computer asks a DNS server for the IP address. The monitor periodically resolves canary domains (Google, Cloudflare, Wikipedia) and compares the answers against tamper-proof Cloudflare DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH). If the IP addresses differ or point to a local private decoy IP, you are warned that your internet is being hijacked!
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Shown directly on the Dashboard inside the Connection Health card. You can also click Check Connection Health for an instant test anytime.

🧱 Part 2: The Core Defensive & Monitoring Foundation

📻 Feature 6: 100% Passive Wi-Fi Discovery Engine

CORE FOUNDATION
💡 What Is It?
The underlying engine that quietly discovers all nearby Wi-Fi networks (SSIDs, MAC addresses, signal strength, channel frequencies, and security protocols) using native Windows wireless services.
⚙️ How Does It Work?
Instead of sending noisy packets, it uses the Windows WLAN query netsh wlan show networks mode=bssid. Like an FM radio, it simply listens to public beacon frames broadcasted voluntarily by nearby routers. It requires zero extra hardware or custom drivers.
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Runs automatically in the background. Visible in real time under Live Scanner.

🧮 Feature 7: Explainable Multi-Factor Risk Engine (0–100 Scoring)

RISK SCORING
💡 What Is It?
An intelligent scoring algorithm that grades every network's safety on a clear 0 (Completely Safe) to 100 (Extremely Dangerous) scale, with plain-English explanations of why points were added.
⚙️ How Does It Work?
Evaluates protocol security (Open +85, WEP +75, WPA +35, WPA2 +10, WPA3 0), cipher algorithms (TKIP +20), hidden SSIDs (+15), default names (+15), and sudden security changes (+30). Categorizes networks into 4 simple tiers: LOW (0–29), MEDIUM (30–59), HIGH (60–79), and CRITICAL (80–100).
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Color-coded badges in Live Scanner, Security Monitor, and executive reports.

⏳ Feature 8: Three-State Lifecycle Tracking & Smart Debouncing

ANTI-FALSE-ALARM
💡 What Is It?
A state tracking engine that remembers whether a network is genuinely present, temporarily flickering due to radio interference, or completely gone.
⚙️ How Does It Work?
Uses a 3-strike rule: ACTIVE (currently visible), TEMPORARILY_MISSING (missed 1 scan, waiting to confirm), and NOT_DETECTED (missed 3 consecutive scans, officially gone). This debouncing stops 99% of false alarms caused by temporary radio flickers.
📍 Where To Find It In The App: Tracked in the status column of Security Monitor and in the History view.

🏷️ Feature 11: Offline Hardware Vendor (OUI) Resolver

HARDWARE FORENSICS
💡 What Is It?
Identifies the real physical manufacturer of any Wi-Fi router (e.g. Apple, Cisco, TP-Link, Intel, Raspberry Pi) without needing an internet connection.

❓ Part 4: Frequently Asked Questions & Master Summary

❓ Can this tool hack into or disconnect my neighbor's Wi-Fi?
No. This is a 100% defensive and passive security application. It never transmits deauthentication frames, never injects packets, and never attempts to crack passwords. It strictly acts as a protective shield and audit tool.
❓ Does the tool require an internet connection to work?
No. The core scanner, offline vendor OUI database, risk scoring engine, baseline system, SQLite database, and Windows toast alerts operate 100% offline. Internet is only used if you optionally enable Telegram notifications or the DoH canary check.
❓ What should I do if the tool gives me a CRITICAL Evil Twin alert?
Immediately disconnect from that Wi-Fi network! Check your router hardware. If someone nearby is broadcasting a fake copy of your Wi-Fi name, do not enter passwords or sensitive credentials until the rogue access point disappears.
❓ Why does the tool flag my home router for having a 'Default Router Name'?
If your Wi-Fi is named 'TP-Link_XXXX' or 'JioFiber-XXXX', an attacker knows exactly what brand you own and can guess default administrative passwords. Renaming your network to something unique (like 'Skyline_Guest') fixes this issue immediately.
